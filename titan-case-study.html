Titan: why review work leaves the platform
and where AI actually helps

A diagnosis-first approach to a workflow-bypass problem, a notification problem, and a leadership mandate for agentic AI.

The problem

Why does 60% of review work leave the intended workflow?

Three signals were handed over, plus a mandate to put agentic AI into the product this year. None of them was yet a diagnosis.

40%
of completed work items reviewed inside Reviewer Cockpit
60%
approved via email or spreadsheets, outside the platform
+22%
QoQ rise in notification tickets, split evenly between "too many" and "I missed something important"
Signals ≠ diagnosis. 40% Cockpit usage isn't automatically bad, and "too many" and "missed important" aren't automatically opposites. I treated all three as evidence to interrogate, not problems to immediately fix.

What I'd investigate first

Five specific questions, not generic discovery

1
Adoption or capability gap?
Different fixes either way
2
Internal or client bypass?
Different owners entirely
3
Notifications severity-blind?
Same urgency treatment for everything?
4
How long do queries block?
Thinking time vs. forgotten
5
Does work return to the platform?
Audit risk vs. cosmetic
Question 4 carries the most weight. It's the only one tied to a hard, measurable constraint rather than a guess.

Working hypotheses

Two distinct hypotheses, one underlying gap

On Cockpit bypass

More likely an adoption or narrow-capability gap than a missing feature. The workflow already has hard gates against open queries and review points, so something specific is pushing people around it.

On notifications

Not a volume problem but a severity-blindness problem. Everything in a category fires the same way regardless of urgency, so people feel overwhelmed and still miss the one that mattered.

Both point to one gap: the platform has the data to know what matters, but isn't yet using it to help people act differently. That's where agentic AI has a real job.

Biggest bet · Q2 only

Two-quarter roadmap

Q1: diagnose and prototype the safest bet. Q2: earn the rest.

Q1

  • Cockpit: adoption vs. capability; segment the 60%
  • Notifications: test the severity-blindness hypothesis
  • AI: prototype Priority 1 (pre-submission nudge)

Q2 · conditional on Q1 evidence

  • Query still the bottleneck → build Priority 2
  • Cross-job gap validated → invest in Priority 3
  • Neither validated → build neither, revisit

Success metrics

Tied to the actual bottleneck, not generic PM metrics

Outcome% of eligible reviews completed end-to-end in-platform, with no external approval step. The completed action, not Cockpit logins.
Query resolutionMedian time an open query blocks job progression. Directly tied to the one hard, provable gate in the workflow.
Attention quality% of high-priority items acted on within expected SLA. Tests whether severity-blindness is actually improving.
GuardrailMissed or overdue high-priority work and notification-related tickets must not rise while this ships.

The quick-fix decision

"2 weeks" vs. "a full quarter"

The test: does the quick fix meaningfully reduce customer pain without creating debt that blocks the real fix?

Yes: ship it now

Ship as an explicit, named stopgap alongside the quarter-long fix, not instead of it, and tell stakeholders the real fix still ships as planned.

No: don't ship it

If it only hides the symptom or adds complexity the real fix must unwind, don't spend the capacity, and be transparent about why waiting is faster overall.

Where I'd go from here

The product should evolve from a system that tells users what happened into one that helps them understand what matters and act on it.

What I'd validate before committing further

  • Whether Cockpit bypass is really adoption or a specific, nameable capability gap
  • Whether severity-blindness actually explains the missed-important tickets
  • Whether query-blocking time justifies Priority 2 before the cross-job hypothesis is tested
